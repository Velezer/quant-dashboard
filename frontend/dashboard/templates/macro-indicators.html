WHAT CAN MOVE THE MARKETS

Catalyst watch

Key drivers and release events for each instrument. Relationships are conditional and can reverse during fast markets.

PRECIOUS METAL · SAFE HAVEN & RATES

XAUUSD · Gold / U.S. dollar

  • Real yields and the Fed path. Rate repricing changes the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold. Compare 10-year TIPS, the 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields.
  • U.S. dollar and inflation pricing. A stronger dollar can weigh on dollar-priced gold; shifts in inflation compensation can change the real-rate picture. Track the broad dollar and 10-year breakeven.
  • Haven demand and liquidity. Wars, sanctions, trade shocks, banking/fiscal stress and sharp risk repricing can lift haven demand. VIX is only an equity-volatility proxy, not a direct gold-risk measure.
  • Official and investor flows. Central-bank reserve purchases, gold ETF creations/redemptions, and CFTC futures positioning can amplify or offset rate moves.
  • Physical market. China and India buying, jewelry demand, mine supply, recycling and seasonal demand can shift the balance.

PRECIOUS & INDUSTRIAL METAL

XAGUSD · Silver / U.S. dollar

  • Gold, rates and the dollar. Silver often follows gold and can react more sharply to shifts in real yields and the dollar. Compare the TIPS yield, broad dollar and gold market context.
  • Manufacturing cycle and PMI. New orders, production and prices in U.S. ISM, China NBS/Caixin, and global manufacturing surveys can change demand expectations. Follow the charted China official manufacturing PMI and the official ISM Manufacturing PMI reports; the Federal Reserve production chart is a separate output series, not PMI.
  • Technology and industry. Solar photovoltaic cells, electronics, data centers, EVs and grid investment affect industrial demand; silver thrifting or substitution in solar can offset higher installation volumes.
  • Mine supply and available metal. Much silver is produced as a byproduct of other metals. Mine disruptions, recycling, inventories, lease rates and physical-market tightness can move supply expectations.
  • Flows and positioning. Silver ETF flows, COMEX stocks, futures positioning and the gold/silver ratio can magnify trend changes.

ENERGY · GLOBAL SUPPLY & DEMAND

UKOUSD · Brent crude

  • OPEC+ decisions. Production quotas, compliance, voluntary cuts, spare capacity and official guidance reset expected supply.
  • Geopolitics and transport. Sanctions, conflict, outages, weather and shipping risk near Hormuz, the Red Sea/Suez or other routes can add or remove a risk premium.
  • Weekly balances. EIA U.S. crude and product stocks, production, imports/exports and refinery runs can signal a tighter or looser balance. The weekly crude stocks chart excludes the Strategic Petroleum Reserve.
  • Demand and growth. China activity, global manufacturing PMI, transport demand and refinery margins can change the demand outlook. Compare the charted China NBS Manufacturing PMI, U.S. manufacturing output, and official ISM PMI releases.
  • Benchmark and market stress. Compare Brent with WTI; monitor oil volatility, futures-curve spreads and CFTC positioning. Spot benchmarks are context, not an intraday broker CFD quote.
  • Macro overlay. Dollar strength, rates and recession risk can move demand expectations and USD-priced crude. Track the broad dollar.
